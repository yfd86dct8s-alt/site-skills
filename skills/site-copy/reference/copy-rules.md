# Copy rules

## Principles

- Clarity over cleverness. If the reader must decode the line, it failed. A cold visitor should know what
  you do, for whom, and what to do next within five seconds.
- Specific over vague: "Cut your weekly reporting from 4 hours to 15 minutes" beats "save time". For a
  service business, specific = the situation, the place, the timeline, the next step. Only if true and
  provable (see `regulated-claims.md`).
- Customer language over company language. Lift phrases from reviews, call notes, forum threads. A person
  with a flooded basement writes "water in my basement", not "water mitigation services".
- Benefit before feature; one idea per section; active voice; plain words ("use", not "utilize").
- Confident, not qualified: cut "very", "really", "almost". Exclamation points: none.
- Visitor's side: write from their situation and fear, not our history. Test: could the line be said
  by the visitor about themselves? "We are a full-service firm" fails; "You were hurt and the insurer
  keeps calling" passes. Never headline a section with what only serves us.

## Headline, subhead, CTA

- Headline = the single most important message, specific. Formulas: "{Outcome} without {pain}",
  "The {category} for {audience}", "{Question naming the pain}". Run the "Now you can ..." test: if the
  headline cannot finish that sentence, it is a slogan.
- Subhead: 1-2 sentences that add specificity (who, where, how fast), not a restated headline.
- Hero arc: current discomfort -> better state -> the path (the CTA).
- CTA label = verb + what they get: "Get My Free Case Review", "Check My Roof Estimate". Never "Submit",
  "Learn More", "Click Here", "Get Started".
- **One CTA label per intent, site-wide.** The same action is worded the same on the hero, sticky bar,
  service pages, footer and form button, so the visitor learns one path and analytics group cleanly.
  A secondary action (call) gets its own fixed label ("Call 555-0100") and stays visually secondary.
- Meta: title ~50-60 chars with the main query first; description ~150-160 chars with the reason to
  click (see site-seo `technical-seo.md`). Write them from the visitor's query, not the brand slogan.

## No AI tells (never write these)

1. Contrast reveals: "It's not X, it's Y", "Not just X, but Y". State Y and why.
2. Negation lists: "No fees, no forms, no waiting." Say what does happen; one true absence, once, near
   the CTA ("No obligation").
3. Trailing pile-ons: a finished claim followed by more comma clauses. End at the claim.
4. Self-answered questions and colon reveals: "The result? Faster." Just say it. A real visitor question
   as an FAQ heading or the reader's own pain question is fine.
5. Stock openers: "In today's fast-paced world", "Whether you're X or Y", "Say goodbye to",
   "Unlock the power of", "X, reimagined", "Take it to the next level".
6. Em dashes in headlines, subheads, CTAs, meta text.
7. Significance inflation and empty vocabulary (seamless, robust, powerful, cutting-edge, tailored,
   comprehensive, premier): replace with the fact it stands in for.
8. Rule of three on autopilot and staccato drama fragments: at most one list of three and one fragment
   per section.
9. Unsourced social proof and weasel attribution ("many clients say", "studies show"): cite or cut.
10. False ranges ("from startups to enterprises", "from X to Y") that name no real span: say who.
11. Synonym cycling (client / customer / partner for the same person): pick one term, keep it.
12. Actorless passive and inanimate subjects with human verbs ("mistakes are avoided", "our process ensures"):
    name who does it.
13. Chatbot closers and generic positive endings ("Ready to take the next step?", "the future is bright"):
    end on the concrete next action.

Do not flag: a real list of three, a single contrast that is true, plain common words ("help", "fast") used
literally, the client's own idioms. Do not over-sterilize: copy with no voice is as machine-made as slop. Keep the client's real speech
rhythm, a concrete detail, one human sentence per section.

## Swap test and gaps

- If a line works unchanged on a competitor's site, rewrite it. If the differentiator is unknown, flag
  `[NEED: differentiator]` and ask; do not fill with adjectives.
- Fabricated statistics, testimonials, reviews, names, case results: never. Placeholders in a build are
  "not connected / not provided" states, not demo data.

## Self-check before delivering

1. Search the draft for each pattern above; read every sentence over 20 words; read aloud.
2. Swap test and visitor-side test on every headline.
3. Every number/promise has a ledger row (or is removed). Weak numbers (small review/client counts)
   omitted; keep only the strong figure, and sweep every occurrence including meta, alt text, schema.
4. One CTA label per intent; no "Submit".
5. Voice matches `DESIGN.md`: headlines may be bolder, body clearer, CTAs plainest.
